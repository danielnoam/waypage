The Quiet Craft of Bookbinding

By Iris Bennett

A hand-bound book begins as a stack of large sheets, folded into sections and pressed flat overnight. Each section is pierced along its fold and sewn to the next with linen thread, so the book opens flat and lasts for centuries.

The cover comes last: boards wrapped in cloth or leather, a ribbon, perhaps a title stamped in gold. Binders say you can tell a good book by how it falls open in your hand.
